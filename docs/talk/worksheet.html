Key terms

Toy Spider cipher: the ten-card, mod 10 version on this sheet. The full Spider cipher uses all 40 cards, mod 40, with letters and padding.

Plaintext (p): the message you mean.

Ciphertext (c): the scrambled message you send.

Key: the shared secret; here, the starting order of the deck.

Symmetric key: sender and receiver use the same key, one to encrypt, the other to decrypt.

Pad: the digit read from the deck and added to p; a new one for every digit.

Mod 10: arithmetic that wraps around: 7 + 5 ≡ 2.

Cut: move cards from the top to the bottom as one block.

Back-front shuffle: deal into a new pile, alternating on top and underneath.

State: what the cipher remembers between digits: the order of the deck.

Strong: attacking it is infeasible for every reasonable future model of computation.

Permutation: an ordering, or rearrangement, of the cards.

Group: a set of moves you can combine, including “do nothing,” where every move can be undone.

Symmetric group Sn: all n! rearrangements of n cards. (No relation to “symmetric key.”)

Graph: dots (vertices) joined by arrows (edges).

Cayley graph: a group drawn as a graph: a dot for each element, an arrow for each chosen move.

Vertex transitive: the graph looks the same from every dot.

Connected: you can walk from any deck to any other.

Free depth: the longest length for which different messages always lead to different decks.

Girth: the length of the shortest loop in the graph.

4 Send your partner a secret Slides 7–10

Start from the key order. The sender picks a secret 4-digit number, encrypts it, and passes over only the ciphertext. The receiver decrypts it. Then both reset to the key order, swap roles, and use the second pair of tables.

5 Reverse your suit Slide 18

6 Think about it

a. Why does the receiver cut on top + p, and not top + c?

b. How many different keys are there for ten cards? For the full 40?

c. Is reversing ten cards an even or an odd number of swaps?

Answers. 2: after the cut 3 4 5 6 7 8 9 Q A 2; after the shuffle 2 Q 8 6 4 3 5 7 9 A. 5: 9 8 7 6 5 4 3 2 A Q. 6a: the sender cut on top + p, and the receiver knows p as soon as they subtract, so their decks stay the same. 6b: 10! = 3,628,800; 40! ≈ 8.2 × 1047 ≈ 2159. 6c: odd: 5 swaps.
