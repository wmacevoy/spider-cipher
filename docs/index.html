The Spider cipher

A self has an inside.

Others are outside it. What crosses between is yours to choose.

Attention chooses what comes in. A secret chooses what goes out.

The Spider cipher exists so that anyone, with a deck of cards and someone they trust, can choose which secrets they keep and which they share. No device, no account, no one to ask. Forty cards on a table, and an order of them that only the two of you know.

How it works

  1. Agree, in person, on a key: an order of the cards.
  2. For each digit, read a pad from the deck and add it.
  3. Cut the deck on the card the message chooses, then shuffle.
  4. Your partner subtracts instead of adding. Your decks stay in step.

Everything about the cipher is public. Only the order of your deck is secret.

Strong, and checkable

A scheme is strong when attacking it is infeasible for every reasonable future model of computation. Spider is meant to meet that bar by hand. The argument is in the open, with the proofs, the computer searches, and the questions still unanswered, so it can be checked rather than trusted.

It will tell you if a message was changed on the way. Every message ends in a row of five of the same card; change any card before it and the decks fall out of step, the row never comes, and the reader knows. What it won’t do is carry the key for you.
